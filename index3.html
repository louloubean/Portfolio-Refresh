Project Presence

Description

Project Presence is a service concept for phone-free, analog skill workshops that help overstimulated people reclaim focus, learn tangible skills, and reconnect with others in real life.

My Role

I co-led product and service definition, synthesized user interviews, contributed to the competitive and TAM/SAM/SOM analysis, and helped build the financial model and use-of-funds story for our pitch.

Date

Graduate product management studio · Fall 2025 (IDSN 599, USC Iovine & Young Academy).

View Case Study

Problem Definition

We started with a broad provocation around a “life less digital,” mapping how distraction, burnout, and constant notifications erode focus and connection. From there, we framed a design challenge: how might we create intentional, phone-free spaces for people to reconnect with themselves and others?

Ideation

We explored multiple concepts—from digital sabbaticals to guided retreats—and clustered them into themes. The most compelling direction was a membership-based “analog skill gym” where people build real-world skills while practicing presence.

Interviews & Personas

Semi-structured interviews with students, therapists, and tech professionals surfaced a common pattern: people feel over-scheduled and overstimulated, but still crave learning and community. These insights shaped our Guide persona (skilled host) and Maker personas (overloaded learners).

Market Research

Once we had a promising direction, we looked outward—analyzing adjacent markets and competitors to understand where Project Presence could credibly live and differentiate.

Competitive Landscape

We mapped wellness retreats, maker workshops, and digital detox experiences across price and perceived quality. This revealed white space for local, repeatable skill workshops that deliver retreat-like depth without the travel or premium pricing.

SWOT

Our SWOT highlighted strengths in digital-free environments, hands-on learning, and mental health benefits, alongside risks like cost, scheduling friction, and dependence on skilled hosts. External threats include a crowded wellness market and easily copyable formats.

Market Sizing

Using wellness tourism and digital detox estimates, we sized a large global TAM and a focused SAM for unplugged, skill-based retreats. Applying conservative penetration and realistic spend per participant yielded a SOM in the $10–50M range for Project Presence.

Value Proposition

With the opportunity and constraints defined, we refined a clear, testable value proposition that ties together emotional, functional, and business value.

Unique Value Proposition

For overstimulated people who want a “life less digital,” Project Presence offers phone-free, hands-on workshops that help them reclaim focus, learn tangible skills, and reconnect in real life. Unlike one-off retreats, our membership model builds ongoing community so presence becomes a repeatable practice—not just a weekend escape.

Financial Use of Funds

Finally, we translated the concept into numbers—showing how initial capital would be deployed and how the model performs under conservative growth assumptions.

Use of Funds

We modeled an initial raise that front-loads investment into heavy equipment—kilns, saws, sewing machines, and materials—to outfit our first “analog skill gym.” Remaining funds support launch operations, staffing, and marketing so that each additional workshop runs on relatively low marginal cost.

Pro Forma

Our three-year pro forma tests different pricing, utilization, and membership growth scenarios. Even with conservative assumptions, Project Presence pays back its initial investment in year one and shows steady single-digit CAGR, signaling a concept that can sustain both mission and margin.
